# Neesmo viral rules

These come from real uploads, not theory. The channel's own `neesmo/lessons.md` wins when it disagrees:
it is measured on this audience.

## 1. The script formula (from explainers with 10M+ views)
1. **Sentence 1 is a paradox** about something everyday or famous, then a question at once.
   ("A bee shouldn't be able to fly. So why does it?")
2. **Correct a wrong belief**: "It isn't X, it's Y."
3. **Put the viewer in a scene**: "Imagine you're..."
4. **Mechanism, step by step**: the picture changes every 1-2 seconds, with labels and live numbers on screen.
5. **Exact, sourced facts**: numbers, a named person, a book, a paper. If you can't source it, cut it.
6. **Payoff that flips the opening**, then a named-persona line: "Send this to someone who..."
7. **Seamless loop**: the last sentence runs into the first, so a replay sounds continuous.

Hook in the first 2 seconds. Plain spoken words. One idea per line.

## 2. Story channels (lore, history, fiction)
- Subject: a famous, tragic figure told as a betrayal, a sacrifice or a fall. These passed 1,000 views in a
  day on a new channel; dry facts did not.
- First line names the hero and the hook; one short line per beat; a gut-punch line just before the end; a quiet
  last line that lands the emotion.
- Every few videos, a little-known story: it keeps the channel from repeating itself and still holds up.

## 3. Titles, captions, pinned comment
- Title under 70 characters: the name in CAPS + an emotion word or a curiosity gap + one emoji.
  ("He KNEW He Would Die... and Went Anyway 😢")
- Shorts: end the title with `#shorts`. Description: 2-3 sentences, a question, then 3-5 hashtags.
- Pinned comment: a question that splits people (monster or victim? which side are you on?). No engagement bait
  ("like if you...").
- Write 3 hooks and 3 titles, keep the strongest. Use the `viral-hooks` and `viral-captions-and-ctas` skills.

## 4. The look
- Dark background, one accent colour, big readable shapes.
- Small captions at the TOP third with the key word highlighted (not a banderole at the bottom).
- Every named thing gets its own moving explainer scene (a map drawing itself, objects dropping on a scale,
  a counter running). Never a still picture with a label alone. Characters and creatures always move.
- Keep labels and arrows out of the caption band.
- Optional small corner mascot. Calm narrator voice.

## 5. Length and sound
- Shorts / Reels: 45-55 s (story) or up to ~85 s (explainer that holds). Escalate every 8-10 s.
- Music quiet under the voice (about 30 %, ducked further while the voice speaks). Loudness about -14 LUFS.

## 6. Quality gate before anything goes to the owner
- Right size (1080x1920 vertical for shorts, 1920x1080 for long), audio present, loudness about -14 LUFS.
- A contact sheet of ~12 frames: subject visible in every scene, nothing over a caption or a face, every word
  spelled right, nothing cut off at the edges.
- Every fact in `SOURCES.md` with a link.

## 7. What to measure (the analyst's job)
- Shorts: percentage viewed and "stayed to watch" (vs swiped away) matter most, then views at 48 h, then
  subscribers per 1,000 views.
- Long videos: average view duration and click-through rate of the thumbnail.
- Keep about 80 % of videos on what is proven and 20 % experiments, so the channel doesn't get stuck.
- Change one thing at a time (topic, hook type, length, look) so a lesson is clear.
