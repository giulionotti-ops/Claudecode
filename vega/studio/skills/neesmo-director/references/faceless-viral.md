# Faceless and voiceless shorts that go viral

Patterns seen in faceless engineering / science / "how it works" accounts (cartoon or code-rendered
animation, no face, often no voice, millions of views). Use them when you design the shot list.

## The hook is a picture, not a sentence
- Frame 1 already shows the problem: a machine about to fail, a person about to fall, an impossible object,
  a number counting up. No logo, no title card, no "did you know".
- The question appears as 2-5 words of on-screen text over the picture ("What if it breaks?").
- Motion starts on frame 1. A still first frame loses the scroll.

## A new picture every 1-2 seconds
- Cuts, camera moves, UI cards popping in, a label drawing on, a counter ticking: something new every 1-2 s.
- Escalate every 8-10 s: bigger number, worse failure, higher stakes, a twist.

## Captions are the voice
- Phrase-by-phrase captions, 1-4 words at a time, big, centred in the safe area, never full sentences.
- One keyword per phrase in the channel accent colour; pop/scale it in on the beat.
- Voiceless mode: captions + SFX + music carry everything. Read time is ~0.3 s per word plus 0.4 s.

## UI cards and diagrams pop in
- Labels with leader lines, spec cards (name, number, unit), arrows, progress bars, warning icons.
- Cards pop with overshoot (scale 0 -> 1.1 -> 1.0 in 8 frames) and a UI "pop" sound.
- Numbers count up live, never appear static.

## Code-rendered cartoons
- Clean flat shapes, thick outlines, 3-4 colours + accent, soft rim light, a subtle drop shadow.
- Characters and creatures always move (walk cycles, breathing, blinking); machines always run.
- Cross-sections and x-ray reveals show how a thing works inside.

## Sound sells it
- Every pop, every cut, every move has a sound. Whooshes on moves, clicks on labels, impacts on reveals.
- A low bed (hum, wind, room tone) under everything so silence never feels empty, except the one
  deliberate silence before the climax.
- Music: steady pulse, drops on the payoff, cuts dead on the cliffhanger.

## Two house styles that work (studied frame by frame)

### A. "How does X work?" x-ray explainer (engineeredwithin1: drill, hair clipper)
- Frame 1: the object in real use in its real place (clipper on a head at the barber, drill in a wall), a title
  banner `HOW DOES A <X> WORK?` with the object word in the accent colour, and one wild real number under it
  (`7,200 RPM: 240 SNIPS A SECOND`, `15,360 RPM: FASTER THAN F1`).
- A HUD that never leaves the top of the frame: a time-scale badge (`REAL SPEED` -> `256x SLOWER` -> `128x`
  -> ... -> `REAL SPEED`) and a live RPM/counter gauge. The video slows time down to look inside, then speeds back
  up to real speed at the end. That ramp IS the story arc and the loop.
- Numbered question chapters: a red number disc + banner (`1 WHAT SPINS IT?`, `2 WHY GEARS?`, `3 WHY A SLOT?`)
  with a short answer strip under it (`COILS PULL A MAGNET`, `ONLY SIDEWAYS GETS THROUGH`). 4-5 chapters.
- Every chapter goes one layer deeper: cutaway shell, x-ray see-through casing, exploded view sliding apart,
  a white circle "magnifier" ring over the part that matters, leader-line labels (`MAGNET`, `CHIP`, `ONE HAIR`).
- Cel-shaded toy-like 3D: one bold product colour (yellow, red), dark navy background, soft rim light,
  vintage marquee banners for text. Tiny watermark only.
- Recap shot `ALL TOGETHER` / chain label `BATTERY > MOTOR > PIN > YOKE > BLADE`, the whole machine labelled.
- Climax: the counter ramps (`60 SNIPS A SECOND` -> `120` -> real speed) with rings pulsing, then the last
  frame returns to frame 1 exactly. Perfect loop.
- Voiceless: banners + labels + SFX carry it. Length 28-34 s.

### B. Editorial paper-cutout explainer (belltakesocial: war money, drones)
- Palette locked to 3 colours: signal red, cream paper, ink black. Serif captions, 2 lines, centred low-middle,
  ONE word in the accent red per caption (`money`, `hundred`, `hunt`, `partnership`).
- Torn-paper reveals: a ragged cream paper hole opens on the red background to show each new prop.
- Props are documents: calendar page (`JANUARY 2022`), share certificates, newspaper/legal clippings with a
  highlighted line, a crate stamped `AID`, a map with dotted flight paths.
- Data cards with sources: small cream tags `~ $2,000,000` in red + a tiny grey source line ("Pentagon
  estimates"). Cost-vs-cost pairs ($2,000,000 missile vs $50,000 drone) are the hook engine.
- Unit charts: 620 dots for 620 interceptors next to 620 drone icons; rows of icons for "every one".
- Line-art drawings (gun, drone, warship) on dark night scenes with flat smoke shapes; light rain lines.
- Structure: a provocative 2nd-person question ("So you want money from war?") then numbered steps
  (`Step one:` ... `Step twelve:`, `Last step:`) with a giant step number sliding in.
- Ending: a poll card ("Who profits most from war?" with 4 options) to pull comments, or a dry punchline
  ("Call it partnership. Smile.").
- Narrated or caption-led; works at 1 min and at 2-3 min.

### What to steal from both
- A persistent on-screen device (HUD, step counter, time-scale badge) that tracks progress: people stay to see
  it finish.
- Numbered chapters/steps phrased as questions the viewer already has.
- One real number per beat, shown as a designed card, never just said.
- One locked art direction per series (palette, type, texture), so every video is recognisable in 0.5 s.
- End by returning to frame 1 or by asking the viewer to vote.

## Endings that bring people back
- Payoff answers the hook in the last 3 s. Last frame composes into frame 1 so the loop is seamless.
- "Choose your path" sequels: end on a choice ("Part 2: what if it was underwater?") and let comments
  decide the next video. The marketer gets the top comment from the analyst.
- Series formats (same opening shot, same music cue, a different object each time) build habit.

## What kills them
- Talking-head pacing with no picture change for 3+ s.
- Text in the top third (covered by the app UI) or the bottom 20 % (covered by the caption/buttons).
- Generic stock-like motion with no idea per shot.
- A hook that the video never pays off.
