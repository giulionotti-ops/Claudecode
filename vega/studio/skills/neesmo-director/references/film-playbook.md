# Film playbook: steal like a director

Every shot names one reference: `"reference": "Film (Director, year) - technique"`. Borrow the
*technique*, never the characters, logos or footage. Pick by what the beat must make the viewer feel.

## Hook (first 1.5 s): stop the thumb
- **Jaws (Spielberg, 1975)** - dolly zoom on the face at the moment of realisation. Use for "wait, what?".
- **Saving Private Ryan (Spielberg, 1998)** - handheld, shutter-chop, debris in the air: drop the viewer into chaos with no warm-up.
- **Gravity (Cuarón, 2013)** - a tiny figure tumbling against something enormous; silence + breathing.
- **The Dark Knight (Nolan, 2008)** - open on the action already in progress; no title, no set-up.
- **Up (Docter, 2009)** - one silent montage that tells a life: start with the ending image, then rewind.
- **2001: A Space Odyssey (Kubrick, 1968)** - centred one-point composition + a single deep tone: dread in one frame.

## Scale and awe
- **Arrival / Dune (Villeneuve, 2016 / 2021)** - minuscule observer, monolithic object, flat overcast light, low drone.
- **Interstellar (Nolan, 2014)** - the camera fixed to the vehicle hull; the universe moves past. Organ swell.
- **Lawrence of Arabia (Lean, 1962)** - extreme wide, a dot on the horizon slowly becoming a figure.
- **Planet Earth (BBC)** - macro to aerial in one cut: the tiny thing and its world.
- **Oppenheimer (Nolan, 2023)** - IMAX close on a face, then the event; sound arrives late.

## Mechanism and explanation (engineering, science)
- **Fight Club (Fincher, 1999)** - the IKEA catalogue: labels and prices pop into a real room. Labels with live numbers.
- **Panic Room (Fincher, 2002)** - the impossible camera that flies through keyholes and walls: travel *through* the machine.
- **The Martian (Scott, 2015)** - step-by-step problem solving, each step a clean insert, counter on screen.
- **Iron Man (Favreau, 2008)** - holographic wireframe exploded view, parts float apart and lock back.
- **Ocean's Eleven (Soderbergh, 2001)** - the plan told in quick cutaways while the voice explains; then reality.
- **The Big Short (McKay, 2015)** - break the frame: a direct simple analogy to explain a complex system.
- **Apollo 13 (Howard, 1995)** - control-room inserts: gauges, switches, numbers; tension from data alone.
- **Chernobyl (Renck, 2019)** - flat cold light, dosimeter clicking; the invisible danger made audible.

## Tension and countdown
- **Dunkirk (Nolan, 2017)** - Shepard tone + ticking watch under everything; cross-cut three timelines.
- **Sicario (Villeneuve, 2015)** - sub-bass pulse, silhouettes against a dusk horizon, slow approach.
- **Mission: Impossible (De Palma, 1996)** - the hang: total silence, a single drop of sweat (ECU).
- **No Country for Old Men (Coens, 2007)** - no music at all; amplified small sounds carry the fear.
- **1917 (Mendes, 2019)** - one continuous move; the viewer can't look away because the camera never cuts.

## Speed, energy, montage
- **Hot Fuzz / Baby Driver (Wright, 2007 / 2017)** - ultra-fast insert montage, every cut on a sound (zip, stamp, click), edits on the music beat.
- **Requiem for a Dream (Aronofsky, 2000)** - hip-hop montage: 4-frame ECUs, the same sequence each time, a sound for each.
- **Mad Max: Fury Road (Miller, 2015)** - keep the subject in the centre of every shot so very fast cuts stay readable; crank the frame rate.
- **Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse (Persichetti, 2018)** - halftone dots, on-twos animation, comic text SFX, freeze-frame panels.
- **Snatch (Ritchie, 2000)** - freeze-frame name card per character, snap zoom.

## Emotion, loss, the human beat
- **Schindler's List (Spielberg, 1993)** - all grey, one red object: the single thing that matters.
- **Saving Private Ryan** - the sound drops to a muffled ring after an explosion: shock.
- **Grave of the Fireflies / Spirited Away (Takahata / Miyazaki)** - "ma": a still, quiet pause shot before or after the emotion. Wind, a leaf, nothing else.
- **Gladiator (Scott, 2000)** - hand brushing through wheat, warm golden haze, slow motion: memory, the afterlife.
- **Coco (Unkrich, 2017)** - a warm practical glow on a face in the dark (candle, screen): intimacy.

## Mystery, history, dark stories
- **Se7en (Fincher, 1995)** - scratched, jittering title-card text; desaturated green-yellow; rain bed.
- **Blade Runner / 2049 (Scott 1982, Villeneuve 2017)** - volumetric light through blinds, neon practicals, haze, monochrome orange or teal worlds.
- **The Revenant (Iñárritu, 2015)** - natural light only, wide lens close to the face, breath fogging the lens.
- **Barry Lyndon (Kubrick, 1975)** - candle-lit, slow zoom-out from a detail to a painting-like tableau.
- **The Prestige (Nolan, 2006)** - "are you watching closely?": show the trick, hide the answer, reveal in the last shot.

## Comedy, charm, curiosity
- **The Grand Budapest Hotel (Anderson, 2014)** - perfect symmetry, flat pastel, whip-pans, miniature models.
- **Amélie (Jeunet, 2001)** - green-gold palette, quirky inserts with a counter ("there are 15 people having...").
- **Ratatouille (Bird, 2007)** - the taste flash: abstract colour shapes that pulse with music when a sense fires.

## Endings and loops
- **Inception (Nolan, 2010)** - cut to black on the unanswered question (the spinning top); the "braaam".
- **The Matrix (Wachowskis, 1999)** - bullet-time orbit around the frozen decisive moment.
- **Memento (Nolan, 2000)** - the end is the beginning: the last shot leads straight into the first.
- **Up / Wall-E (Pixar)** - echo an early image at the end with a changed meaning.

## Title and text design (on-screen words)
- **Saul Bass (Vertigo, Psycho, Anatomy of a Murder)** - bold shapes and lines that slice in and assemble the word.
- **Se7en** - jittery hand-scratched type for dread.
- **Stranger Things** - glowing serif letters sliding together, red neon, synth pad.
- **Sherlock (BBC)** - floating text in the scene that tracks with the camera: thoughts, texts, numbers.
