A three-tonne dragon is ten times heavier than the biggest animal that ever flew. It can flap all day and never leave the ground.
So you make your bones hollow, like a bird's. Your whole skeleton gets lighter.
But hollow bones snap. One hard landing and your wing breaks.
So you brace them inside with tiny struts, like a vulture's wing bones. Light as a straw, strong as a bridge.
But you're still far too heavy to flap your way up.
So you don't flap. You climb to the top of a volcano, and you jump.
But gliding only goes one way: down.
So you ride the heat. Hot air rising off the volcano lifts you in circles, like a condor that can soar for five hours without a single flap.
That's why dragons live on volcanoes. Not for the fire. For the lift.
