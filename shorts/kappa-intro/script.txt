Some rivers in Japan still have warning signs. Not for floods. For the kappa.
In Japanese folklore, the kappa is a river creature the size of a child, with a turtle shell, webbed hands, and a beak for a mouth.
It hides in rivers and ponds, and drags horses and people into the water.
But here's the strangest part. On top of its head is a dish of water. That water is its power. If it spills, the kappa grows weak.
So how do you beat one? You bow. A kappa is polite, so it bows back, and the water pours out.
Or you offer it a cucumber, its favorite food.
The real animal behind it? One Edo-period naturalist probably drew his kappa from a softshell turtle. In some regions, the kappa even shares that turtle's name.
