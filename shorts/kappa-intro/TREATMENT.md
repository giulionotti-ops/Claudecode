# Meet the Kappa (intro short)

~53 s vertical short, Fable Physics. Format: **intro** ("Meet the X") for a less known creature, same look as `kraken/`: one-word captions, gold label chips, arrows, rings, a gauge, the Liam voice. Next for this creature: a physics short ("Could a kappa actually work?").

**Culture:** Japan, folklore (a yōkai of rivers and ponds; known as kappa in the east, kawatarō and other names in the west).

## Claims and sources
- **Warning signs about kappa still stand by some Japanese rivers and ponds, mostly to keep children away from the water.** Wikipedia, "Kappa (folklore)" (citing DK 2024): such signs "often merely serve as scary warnings to dissuade young children from playing too close to rivers, ponds, etc." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kappa_(folklore)
- **Child-sized, turtle-like shell, webbed hands and feet, beaked mouth.** Wikipedia (citing Tsutsumi 2024: "about the size of a child"; Foster 2015: "webbed hands and feet, and … a turtle-like carapace"; Ozawa 2011: "a pointed or beaked mouth"). Britannica, "kappa": https://www.britannica.com/topic/kappa-Japanese-mythology
- **Lives in rivers and ponds; drags horses and people into the water.** Wikipedia (Ishida, *Kappa komabikikō*, on the kappa's "steed-tugging"; the Izumo tale of a kawako that tried to draw a horse into the water). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kappa_(folklore)
- **Dish (sara) of water on top of its head; if the water spills, the kappa is weakened.** Wikipedia (citing Okuno 1988, Tsutsumi 2024, Ishikawa 1985: "if the water spills, the kappa is weakened, or it may even die"). japan-guide.com, "Yokai and where to find them: Kappa": https://www.japan-guide.com/blog/home-delivery/200502.html
- **Bow to it; it bows back and spills the water.** Wikipedia (citing Ashkenazi 2003: "if a person makes a deep bow, it will return the gesture"). yokai.jp: https://yokai.jp/en/yokai/kappa
- **Cucumber is its favourite food.** Wikipedia (citing Ashkenazi 2003; Foster 1998 on cucumber offerings). Britannica: throwing a cucumber into the water where kappa live is a standard way to placate them.
- **An Edo-period naturalist probably drew his kappa from a softshell turtle.** Wikipedia (citing Ozawa 2011): Kurimoto Tanshū's *Senchūfu* kappa is "probably based on a life drawing from a real softshell turtle".
- **In some regions the kappa shares the softshell turtle's name (suppon).** Wikipedia (citing Ishida 2017): "the terrapin-based names are a giveaway that the kappa is locally considered to be very turtle-like."

## Real-animal anchor
- **Chinese/Japanese softshell turtle** (*Pelodiscus sinensis*, suppon): leathery flat shell, long snorkel snout; the only animal link here with a source.
- Left out: the Japanese giant salamander theory (only fan wikis and blogs, no solid source found), and the shirikodama story (not family-friendly).
