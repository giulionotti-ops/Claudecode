# Meet the Mapinguari (intro short)

~53 s vertical intro, Fable Physics. Next for this creature: a physics short ("Could a giant sloth survive in the rainforest?").
**Culture:** Amazon rainforest, Brazil (folklore of Indigenous peoples of Pará, Amazonas, Acre). Told as folklore, with respect. Not a sacred being.

## Claims and sources
- **Folklore of Indigenous peoples of Brazil; forests of Pará, Amazonas, Acre.** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapinguari: "a legend rooted in the folklore of the Indigenous peoples of Brazil".
- **Huge, hairy, foul-smelling.** Same page: "an extremely foul-smelling, hairy animal"; fur "long red, brown, or black hair" (script says only "hairy").
- **Bullets bounce off its skin.** Same page: "effectively bulletproof save only the area near the navel". Script says "bullets bounce off"; "tellings" framing.
- **One big eye; mouth down to the belly.** Same page: "a large, single eye in either the abdomen or the forehead"; "a large mouth split from nose to stomach".
- **Feet point backwards.** Same page: "backwards-oriented donkey-like hooves". (The tracker-fooling detail was cut: no reliable source.)
- **Megatherium ~6 m long, ~4 tonnes.** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megatherium: "a total body length of around 6 metres (20 ft)"; "3,700–4,000 kilograms".
- **Giant sloths extinct ~12,000 years ago.** Same page: "became extinct around 12,000 years ago."
- **Mylodon (cousin) had bony plates in its skin.** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mylodon: "The mylodonts are the only representatives of the sloths to have bony plates embedded in their skin." ("like armour" is a comparison; the page itself calls the armour an inference.)
- **Origin idea (not stated as fact in the script).** Mapinguari page: David C. Oren (1993) suggested it "might be based on recent sightings of an extinct ground sloth"; other scientists criticised this. The script only asks "could one have hidden?".

## Real-animal anchor
Giant ground sloths (Megatherium, Mylodon).
Cut for lack of a source: tracker-fooling backward prints, tail as third support, Monte Verde Mylodon meat.
