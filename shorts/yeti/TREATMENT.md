# Could a yeti actually live in the Himalayas? (physics short)

~53 s vertical short, Fable Physics. Physics format (problem, fix, new problem), same look as `kraken/` and `kappa/`. A familiar creature, so it goes straight to a physics short.

**Culture:** Himalaya (Sherpa and Tibetan folklore). Told as folklore, with respect; the twist says the legend grew from real bears, it does not mock the stories.

## Chain
1. **Legend: a huge, hairy, ape-like walker of the high snow.** Wikipedia "Yeti": large bipedal creature covered in brown, grey or white hair; folklorists trace it to Sherpa folklore; Tibetan lore. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeti
2. **Problem: a big body sinks in deep snow. Fix: wide furry feet like a snowshoe hare.** The hare's large, furred hind feet spread its weight so it stays on top of the snow (National Park Service, Rocky Mountain NP): https://www.nps.gov/romo/learn/nature/snowshoe_hare.htm . "A big body sinks" is the short's own reasoning (weight per foot area, "foot loading").
3. **Problem: thin air. Fix: tuned for low oxygen, like the yak.** Yak genome study: genes under selection in the yak lineage are enriched for hypoxia and nutrition-metabolism pathways. Qiu et al., Nature Genetics 44:946 (2012), "The yak genome and adaptation to life at high altitude": https://research.aber.ac.uk/en/publications/the-yak-genome-and-adaptation-to-life-at-high-altitude/ . We say only "genes built for high altitude"; we do not claim lungs or hemoglobin details (not in the sources we read).
4. **Problem: winter, nothing to eat. Fix: sleep through it on stored fat, like a brown bear.** Bears in dormancy go for months without eating, living on stored fat (NPS Katmai, "Hibernation in Bears": https://www.nps.gov/katm/blogs/Hibernation-in-Bears.htm ; Knowable Magazine, "Bear hibernation": https://knowablemagazine.org/article/living-world/2022/bear-hibernation-more-winters-nap ). Left out: any claim about Himalayan brown bear hibernation dates or marmot diet (unsourced).
5. **Twist: what you built is a bear.** Lindqvist et al., Proc. R. Soc. B, 29 Nov 2017, "Evolutionary history of enigmatic bears in the Tibetan Plateau-Himalaya region and the identity of the yeti" (doi 10.1098/rspb.2017.1804): nine purported yeti samples (bone, tooth, skin, hair, feces); one was a dog, the other eight were Asian bears (Tibetan brown bears, a Himalayan brown bear, an Asian black bear). https://www.buffalo.edu/ubnow/stories/2017/11/lindqvist-yeti-bears.html ; National Geographic: https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/yeti-legends-real-animals-dna-bears-himalaya-science
6. **Name: "yeti" comes from Tibetan words meaning "rock bear".** Wikipedia "Yeti": from Tibetan *g.ya' dred*, *g.ya'* "rocky place" + *dred* "bear". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeti
7. **Close: "the legend grew from a real animal"** is our interpretation of 5-6 (the study suggests the stories are rooted in real bears).

## Real-animal anchors
Snowshoe hare (feet on snow), yak (high-altitude genes), brown bear (hibernation on fat), Asian bears (the DNA twist).

## Cut for lack of a source
"Yeti footprints are melted, enlarged bear prints" (Shipton 1951): the sources we found say the 1951 prints were sharp and deformed by sun and wind, and do not support melt-enlargement. Not used.
