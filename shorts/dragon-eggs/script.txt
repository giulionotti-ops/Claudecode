A dragon's egg has to stay warm for three months. But if a three-ton mother sits on it, she crushes it flat.
So you don't sit on it. You bury it in warm volcanic ash, just like the maleo bird does.
But lava is over a thousand degrees. Bury it too close, and your egg is cooked in seconds.
So you test the ground with your snout, like a malleefowl checking its nest, until you find the perfect spot: thirty-four degrees.
But now your egg is buried under a metre of ash. How does the baby breathe?
So the shell is covered in thousands of tiny pores. Air gets in, and stale air gets out.
But that shell is as hard as stone. The baby is trapped.
So it grows a tiny egg tooth on the tip of its snout, just like a baby crocodile, and chips its way out.
And it never meets its mother. It digs up through the ash alone, shakes itself off, and flies on its very first day.
