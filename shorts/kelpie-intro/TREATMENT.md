# Meet the Kelpie (intro short, Scotland · folklore)
Format: intro. Voice: Liam (ElevenLabs v3). Model: "Horse" by Henry S (CC BY), Walk / Gallop / Swim / Idle clips. Kenney nature kit (CC0) props.

## Claims and sources
1. Water spirit of Scottish lochs, usually a horse: Wikipedia "Kelpie": "a mythical shape-shifting spirit inhabiting lochs in Scotland", "usually described as a grey or white horse-like creature." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelpie
2. Tame on land, gallops into the water with a rider and drowns them: Historic Environment Scotland blog: "The kelpie appeared tame on land but if anyone sat on its back, it would gallop into the water." https://blog.historicenvironment.scot/2018/10/supernatural-creatures-scottish-folklore
3. Shape-shifts into a (handsome) man; water weeds in the hair give it away: Wikipedia "Kelpie": "they may betray themselves by the presence of water weeds in their hair." HES: "usually that of a handsome man".
4. Laird of Morphie bridled a kelpie and made it carry stones to build his castle: Wikipedia "Kelpie": "the Laird of Morphie captured a kelpie and used it to carry stones to build his castle." Electric Scotland, "Graham of Morphie": "throwing a pair of branks over his head." https://electricscotland.com/kids/stories/graham_morphie.htm
5. Burns, "Address to the Deil" (Kilmarnock edition, 1786): "Then, Water-kelpies haunt the foord, / By your direction". https://www.eighteenthcenturypoetry.org/works/cbu86-w0050.shtml ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Address_to_the_Deil
6. Kelpie stories kept children from dangerous water: HES: "The belief in kelpies performed a useful function in deterring children from going near dangerous stretches of water."
