A griffin has six limbs, and no animal with a backbone ever flew with six. So could it take off?
Imagine you're a griffin: a lion's body, an eagle's wings.
Problem one: weight. A lion weighs one hundred and ninety kilograms. Flying birds top out near eighteen. So copy Quetzalcoatlus: ten metres of wingspan, two hundred kilograms.
But wings need an engine. Birds anchor flight muscle on a keel, a blade of breastbone. So grow one.
But how do you launch? Quetzalcoatlus vaulted up on all four limbs. Griffins have four legs.
But wings make limbs five and six. Birds, bats and pterosaurs fly with their arms. None has a spare pair.
So where did griffins come from? Adrienne Mayor says gold miners found Protoceratops skeletons: a beak, a frill.
