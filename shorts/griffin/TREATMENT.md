# Griffin physics short: "Could a griffin actually fly?"
Legend: Greece / Persia / Near East. Herodotus 4.13 (via Aristeas, lost poem *Arimaspea*): griffins guard gold north of the Issedones.
Format: problem -> fix -> new problem. Twist: Adrienne Mayor's (contested) hypothesis that the legend grew from Protoceratops skeletons.

## Claims and sources (helper research, 2026-11-22)
- Griffin guards gold (Herodotus 4.13, from Aristeas): https://cts.perseids.org/read/greekLit/tlg0016/tlg001/perseus-eng2/4.13.1-4.14.3 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arimaspi
- Mayor, *The First Fossil Hunters* (2000): legend "sprang from tales first told by Scythian gold-miners, who... encountered the skeletons of Protoceratops": https://press.princeton.edu/isbn/9780691245607 . Stated in the script as "Adrienne Mayor says" (hypothesis; a 2024 paper disputes the gold link).
- Protoceratops: Mongolia, Late Cretaceous, ~1.8 m long, beaked, bony frill (NHM, AMNH): https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/dino-directory/Protoceratops.html . Script says "a body the size of a lion" -> loosely, no mass figure used (sources disagree 225-400 kg).
- Male lion average 190 kg (San Diego Zoo: "average: 190 kg"): https://ielc.libguides.com/sdzg/factsheets/lions/summary
- Heaviest flying birds: kori bustard males 11-19 kg (Smithsonian NZP), Guinness record 18.14 kg: https://nationalzoo.si.edu/animals/kori-bustard ; https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/70907-heaviest-flying-bird
- Quetzalcoatlus: 10-11 m wingspan, 200-250 kg, "most reliable upper estimates" (Witton & Habib 2010, PLoS ONE): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2981443
- Quad launch of pterosaurs: Habib 2008, Zitteliana B28: https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/12011/
- Keel anchors the flight muscles (bird flight muscle review): https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s12915-021-01105-1
- Birds, bats, pterosaurs evolved flight independently, wings are modified forelimbs: https://ucmp.berkeley.edu/vertebrates/flight/converge.html ; https://www.mun.ca/biology/scarr/Analogy_of_forelimbs.html
