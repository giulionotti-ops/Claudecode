# Could a kappa actually work? (physics short)

~46 s vertical short, Fable Physics. Physics format (problem, fix, new problem), same look as `kraken/` and `kappa-intro/`. Follows the intro short `kappa-intro/`.

**Culture:** Japan, folklore. Legend details come from `kappa-intro/TREATMENT.md` (child-sized, drags horses into water, water in a dish on the head, weak if it spills, bows back when bowed to).

## Chain
1. **Problem: child-sized, drags horses, loses a tug of war.** Legend: Wikipedia "Kappa (folklore)" (Ishida, steed-tugging); size: Britannica "kappa". The tug-of-war line is the short's own reasoning (a child-sized body vs a horse), not a sourced number.
2. **Fix: grab from the water, drag prey in, as a crocodile does.** Nile crocodiles lunge from the water and drag large prey (wildebeest, zebra) into it, then drown it. National Wildlife Federation, "The Beauty in the Beast": https://www.nwf.org/Magazines/National-Wildlife/2008/The-Beauty-in-the-Beast ; San Diego Zoo factsheet (ielc.libguides.com/sdzg/factsheets/nile_crocodile/diet). "Where it can't plant its feet" is the short's own explanation of why water helps.
3. **Problem: drowning takes time, a kappa needs air.** Turtle-like air breather, per the shell and legend (intro sources).
4. **Fix: breathe like a softshell turtle, through mouth lining and skin.** Chinese softshell turtle (*Pelodiscus sinensis*) uses cutaneous plus buccopharyngeal respiration underwater, and its pharyngeal lining has villiform processes as an aquatic respiratory organ for long stays under water. J Exp Biol 2012 (216:1650, "The Chinese soft-shelled turtle Pelodiscus sinensis excretes urea mainly through the mouth"), citing Wang et al. 1989; Arch Histol Cytol 63(2):181 (J-STAGE). Left out: the "snorkel neck" claim (popular sites only).
5. **Problem: it climbs out and wet skin dries fast.** General amphibian/reptile skin water loss; evaporative water loss figures in J Exp Biol 209:3309 (water-holding frog cocoon study).
6. **Fix: store water like the water-holding frog (*Cyclorana platycephala*), in bladder and skin.** The frog absorbs water and stores it in its bladder and skin pockets; a cocoon of shed skin cuts evaporative water loss to 0.8-38% of uncocooned rates. Wikipedia "Water-holding frog": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water-holding_frog ; J Exp Biol 209:3309: https://cob.silverchair.com/jeb/article-pdf/209/17/3309/1256914/3309.pdf ; FrogID: https://www.frogid.net.au/frogs/cyclorana-platycephala
7. **New problem and twist: the kappa keeps its water in a dish on top, where it can spill; a creature built on stored water can't afford to lose it, so bowing (it bows back) costs it everything.** Dish, weakness and bow from Wikipedia "Kappa (folklore)" and yokai.jp (see `kappa-intro/TREATMENT.md`). The "that is why" link is our interpretation, not a claim from a source.

## Real-animal anchors
Nile crocodile (ambush and drag), Chinese softshell turtle (underwater breathing), water-holding frog (stored water).
