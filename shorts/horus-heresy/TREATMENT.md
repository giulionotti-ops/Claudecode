# The Horus Heresy in 60 seconds: treatment

Style: Stained Glass (`styles/stained-glass/STYLE.md`). Vertical 1080×1920, 24 fps, ~55 s, English voice-over with banderole subtitles.

Fan lore short. The narration tells the lore. **Every picture is an original glass design**: an armoured son and a crowned father, with no Games Workshop armour, aquila, faction marks, logos or art. Following the style's secular rule, there are no halos, crosses or saints. The father is a crowned king, not a saint.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **A rose window as a dial.** Twelve petals, lit one by one like hours, from the Crusade to the fall; the centre is the throne. Clear, but a round dial wastes a vertical frame. It also reads as "a cycle", and this story is a fall, not a cycle.
2. **Two windows facing each other.** Father on one wall, son on the other: morning lights the father, evening the son, and their colours meet on the floor. Beautiful, but it's slow to set up, and a short needs a hook in 3 s.
3. **One tall lancet read bottom to top, opening at the top. (Chosen)** The lancet *is* a 9:16 frame. The hook is the climax: father and son in the top medallion, lit, as a crack splits the glass between them. Then the camera drops down the window into the past and climbs back up one medallion at a time, the sun moving from dawn to dusk as the son's life goes by. The native move, glass cracks and lead mends, lands at the turn: the son's gold pieces slide along the leads and come back blood-red. The ending returns to the top under moonlight.

Why 3: the frame shape, the hook and the story's shape (a rise, a fall) all match. The sun's colour carries the arc: blue dawn of the Crusade → white noon of the Warmaster → gold afternoon of the wound → orange dusk of the march → moonlight.

## 2. Logline and arc

The Emperor's most trusted son is corrupted and turns on him, and their duel ends one age and begins a darker one.

- **Setup:** the father finds the son; the son becomes Warmaster (rise, light growing).
- **Turn:** the wound, the whisper, the crack, and the re-leading into red.
- **Ending:** the duel. The son falls, the father is sealed on the throne, the sun goes down, and only the throne pane keeps its own light.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Chartres' narrative lancets (13th c.):** I learn from them that a life is read upward, medallion by medallion, with a border framing it. I take none of their saints or subjects.
- **The *Game of Thrones* title sequence (2011):** I learn its camera grammar, one continuous camera travelling over a crafted object, and how it gives each stop a beat. I take none of its map, music or designs.

## 7. Cue map

- Free time, 60 bpm grid (one beat a second), D Dorian.
- 0–6: organ pedal D plus a high open fifth; one chime (the "light note", A5) each time a pane lights.
- 6–18: harp arpeggios join, rising with each medallion; the light note on M1 and M2.
- 18–25: low drone on C, a timpani roll under the strike, recorder falling.
- 25–27: **hard stop**, silence (only glass creak) before the crack.
- 27–31: crack, then the re-leading clicks as one rising scale, a step per piece. Organ returns in D minor (Aeolian).
- 31–45: timpani pulse every 2 beats, full organ at the duel, cut out on "Horus fell".
- 45–55: **silence**, then a single long-reverb tubular bell under L10; moon pad.

## 9. Subtitles and titles

- Parchment banderole near the bottom (IM Fell English, dark ink), unfurling from the centre, held ≥ max(1.8 s, line + 0.6 s).
- Title: "THE HORUS HERESY" in incised Cinzel capitals on the stone sill under the window, lit by a moving band at 0.3–4.5 s, small enough to leave the crack readable. The end card carries none of the library's credit.
