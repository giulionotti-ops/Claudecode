# Meet the Selkie (intro short)

~51 s vertical intro short, Fable Physics. Next for this creature: a physics short ("Could a selkie actually work?").
**Culture:** Scotland (Orkney and Shetland folklore); similar seal-folk tales in the Faroe Islands and Ireland. Told as folklore, with respect.

## Claims and sources
- **Selkies are seals in the sea who shed their skin to become human on land; many tales collected in Orkney and Shetland.** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selkie: "they are said to live as seals in the sea but shed their skin to become human on land."; "Many of the folk-tales on selkie folk have been collected from the Northern Isles (Orkney and Shetland)."
- **A stolen sealskin traps the selkie on land.** Same page: a man "steals a female selkie's skin ... and compels her to become his wife." Faroese tale of the seal woman (Kópakonan): "without it, she was trapped on land." https://visitfaroeislands.com/dk/about1/myths-legends/the-seal-woman
- **When she finds her skin she returns to the sea.** Same Wikipedia page: "once she discovers her skin, she will immediately return to the sea."
- **Seven tears into the sea.** Same page: "it was rumoured that when she wished to make contact with her male selkie she would shed seven tears into the sea." Script says "the story goes".
- **Seals haul out on rocky shores.** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_seal: "hauled out on rocks, islands, and shoals not far from shore"; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbour_seal: haulout sites "generally rocky areas".
- **Theory: Finnish and Sami people in sealskin kayaks and clothing mistaken for selkies.** Wikipedia "Selkie": "Finnish and Sami women who were misidentified as selkies because of their sealskin kayaks and clothing." Presented as one theory, not fact.

## Real-animal anchor
Grey seal / harbour seal (haul-out behaviour). Cut for lack of a source: big dark human-like eyes, "singing" calls, once-a-year shore night.
