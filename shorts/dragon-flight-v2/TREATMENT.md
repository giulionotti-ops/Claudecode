# How could a 3-tonne dragon actually fly? (remake, physics short)

~58 s vertical short, Fable Physics. Remake of `dragon-flight/` in the game-model style (animated dragon GLB, Kenney rocks, volcano built in code). Same topic, fresh script with the AUTO.md formula: paradox hook, myth fix, everyday scene, fast mechanism, sourced facts, flip payoff, loop.

**Hook / paradox:** every legend puts its dragon on a volcano, and it isn't for the fire.
**Myth fix:** it doesn't need bigger wings; it needs to stop flapping.
**Everyday scene:** jumping off a wall flapping your arms.
**Payoff (loops into the hook):** warm air rises over hot ground, the hottest ground is a volcano. "...Because that's why / every legend puts its dragon on a volcano."

## Chain and sources
1. **Biggest flyer ever: Quetzalcoatlus, ~200 kg, 10-11 m wings.** Mass estimates vary (about 150-250 kg in recent work); wingspan 10-11 m. Wikipedia "Quetzalcoatlus": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quetzalcoatlus . "Fifteen times heavier" = 3,000 kg / 200 kg (the short's own arithmetic; a 3-tonne dragon is the premise).
2. **Hollow bones with criss-cross struts.** "Birds have many bones that are hollow (pneumatized) with criss-crossing struts or trusses for structural strength." Wikipedia "Bird skeleton": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_skeleton . "Light as a straw, strong as a bridge" is a picture, not a measurement.
3. **Vault off the arms (quadrupedal launch), like Quetzalcoatlus probably did.** Habib, M. (2008), "Comparative evidence for quadrupedal launch in pterosaurs", Zitteliana B28: https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/12011/1/zitteliana_2008_b28_09.pdf . Said as "probably".
4. **A glide only goes down; soaring flyers ride rising warm air (thermals).** Wikipedia "Glider (sailplane)" and "Thermal": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glider_(sailplane) , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal
5. **Andean condors flapped ~1% of flight time; one flew 5+ hours (~170 km) without flapping.** Williams et al. 2020, PNAS, doi 10.1073/pnas.1907360117; summary: https://www.sci.news/biology/andean-condor-flight-08638.html
6. **Warm air rises over hot ground; a volcano is the hottest ground.** General thermal physics (source 4); the volcano step is the short's own inference, not a cited study.
