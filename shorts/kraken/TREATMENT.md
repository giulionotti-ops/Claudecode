# Why Nobody Has Ever Seen a Kraken

52 s vertical short, Fable Physics #3. Same style as `dragon-flight`: problem → fix → new problem, one-word captions, gold label chips, arrows, a cutaway, the Liam voice.

**Twist:** krakens aren't hiding from sailors. A giant squid can't breathe in warm surface water, so it stays deep, and the ones that come up are dying.

## Real-world anchors
- **Size:** the biggest squid ever measured, a colossal squid, weighed about 495 kg; giant squid about 275 kg. A ship-grabbing kraken (60 m or more) would be roughly 100× heavier.
- **Ammonia floats it:** giant squid tissue is full of ammonium chloride, which is lighter than seawater, so the animal is close to neutrally buoyant without swimming.
- **Weak blood:** squid blood carries oxygen with hemocyanin, which holds far less oxygen than our haemoglobin, and holds on to it less well as the water warms.
- **Cold, deep water:** giant squid live about 300–1000 m down; cold water dissolves more oxygen.
- **Dinner-plate eyes:** the eyes of giant and colossal squid are about 27 cm across, the largest of any animal.
- **Sperm whales and the glow:** sperm whales eat giant squid. Nilsson et al. (2012) argue the huge eyes evolved to spot the bioluminescence a diving whale sets off, from more than 120 m away.
- **Surface squid are dying:** giant squid found at the surface are usually dying or dead; strandings have been linked to warm water.
