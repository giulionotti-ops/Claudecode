# Selkie (physics short)

~50 s vertical physics short, Fable Physics. Follows `../selkie-intro`.
**Culture:** Scotland (Orkney and Shetland folklore). Told as folklore, with respect.

## Claims and sources (chain: cold -> fat -> breath-hold -> slow heart -> mass problem -> molt twist)
- **Orkney winter sea about 7-8 °C.** https://seatemperature.info/orkney-water-temperature.html (winter average about 7.9 °C, January 7.8 °C).
- **Cold water: cold shock about 1 min, incapacitation about 10 min, hypothermia about 1 hour (the "1-10-1" rule).** US National Weather Service cold-water safety page, archived: https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20210104202403/https://www.weather.gov/safety/coldwater (approximate times; helper got them from a search snippet).
- **Blubber keeps seals warm.** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbour_seal: "Blubber under the seal's skin helps to maintain body temperature."
- **Grey seals feed at 70 m or more.** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_seal (Diet): dives "taken at depths down to 70 m (230 ft) or more".
- **Heart slows to about 4 beats/min on long dives.** Thompson & Fedak 1993, J. Exp. Biol. 174:139, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8440964/ (free-ranging grey seals, Hebrides; heart rate "fell to 4 beats per minute" in long dives).
- **Female grey seal about 155 kg (Great Britain average 154.6 kg).** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_seal.
- **Harbour seals spend a lot of time ashore when molting.** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbour_seal: "Harbor seals must spend a great deal of time on shore when molting, which occurs shortly after breeding."
- **Folklore:** many selkie tales come from Orkney and Shetland; a selkie who finds her skin returns to the sea (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selkie). Used in the intro short.

## Real-animal anchors
Harbour seal (blubber, molt), grey seal (dive depth, diving heart rate, body mass).
