# The Fall of Cadia: treatment

The second film in the series. Style: Stained Glass. Vertical 1080×1920, 24 fps, 52.5 s, voice 1 (bm_lewis, grim treatment).

The same window grammar as *The Horus Heresy* keeps the series recognisable: one tall lancet read bottom to top, the sun as the clock, a crack at the turn, and a pane that keeps its own light at night. The figures are original power-armour and trooper designs in glass, with no Games Workshop art, sculpts or faction symbols.

## Structure
- **Hook:** the climax first again, but in the middle of the window this time. The star fortress hangs over the world as the voice says the gates of hell were held shut.
- **The climb, bottom to top:**
  1. Cadia ringed with pylons, which wake one by one (dawn).
  2. A trooper, and a child handed a rifle (noon).
  3. Abaddon breaking against the walls while twelve tally marks light up, then thrown back on "held" (afternoon, reddening).
  4. A hard silence, then the star fortress drifting in, its core igniting (violet).
  5. The fortress falls on the beat and hits on "planet". The glass cracks, the pylons are blown out of their places, and the world splits (dusk).
- **Ending:** the top medallion. Troopers in the ruins under a ragged banner raise their rifles on "fought on". Night falls, and only the banner keeps its light; a single bell rings on "stands".

## Native moves
Light is the clock; the glass cracks; the pylon pieces leave their leads and stay scattered; a window holds its own light.

## Sound
D Aeolian on organ, harp, chimes, timpani and a recorder lament:
- an omen chord with a flat sixth;
- eight rising chimes as the pylons wake;
- twelve timpani strokes, one per crusade;
- a dread chord for the fortress, swelling into the impact and cut dead on it;
- a timpani march in the ruins;
- a single long bell, then an open fifth.

All foley is synthesized: glass, lead, sliding glass, wind as the fortress falls, iron ticking at night.

## Subtitles
Parchment banderole, IM Fell English. Long lines are condensed so they pass the reading-time check (characters ÷ 15 + 1.5 s); the narration says every word in full.
