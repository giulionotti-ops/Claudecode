# What makes short explainers go viral (lessons from Keyframes' top reels)

Source: frame-by-frame watch (the `watch` skill) of two reels by the Keyframes account,
"How a screen feels your finger" (17.6M views) and "Why you hit keys smaller than your thumb" (10.5M views).
Both are ~85 s, calm narrator, no hard sell. These are patterns, not guarantees.

## The script formula (same in both)
1. **Paradox in sentence one.** Two true facts that seem to clash: "reacts to the lightest brush, ignores a pen pressed hard" / "every key is smaller than your thumb, yet you hit the right letter". Then a question at once: "So what is it actually feeling?"
2. **Kill the wrong belief.** "It isn't feeling pressure. It's measuring the electricity in your body." The viewer learns they were wrong; that is the hook's payoff promise.
3. **An everyday scene.** "Imagine it's winter and you answer a call with gloves on" / "Imagine you're texting a friend". Puts the viewer inside the mechanism.
4. **Mechanism, step by step**, each step one picture. Change the picture every 1-2 s.
5. **Credibility.** Exact numbers (44 px, 320 px), a named person, a book, a patent. Keep sources in a facts file and cite them in the caption.
6. **Payoff that flips the opening.** "Your screen never felt your finger. It felt a tiny bit of charge go missing."
7. **Named-persona share CTA.** "Follow me and send this to someone whose phone never works with wet hands."
8. **Seamless loop.** The last sentence runs straight into the first ("...Your phone screen reacts to the lightest brush-"), so Instagram replays it and the replay counts as watch time. Likely their biggest single trick.

## The visual style
- Dark background, macro/close-up 3D, **one accent colour** (gold) for the thing that matters.
- **Labels and live numbers on screen** (100 → 81 as the finger nears; "96%" on the predicted key).
- **Captions small, at the top**, 1-3 words at a time, with **the key word highlighted** in a box. The centre of the frame stays free for the visual.
- In some reels a **mascot sits small in a corner** and reacts (points, holds head, shrugs) while diagrams fill the frame.
- A calm, curious narrator: it feels like learning something, not an ad.

## Checklist before publishing
- [ ] Sentence 1 is a paradox about something everyone touches daily
- [ ] A wrong belief is named and corrected within 10 s
- [ ] Every 1-2 s the picture changes; every step has a label or a number
- [ ] At least one exact, sourced fact (number, name, document)
- [ ] The ending flips the opening, then a named-persona "send this to..." line
- [ ] The final words lead back into the first words (loop)
- [ ] Captions at the top with highlighted keywords; centre left for the visual
